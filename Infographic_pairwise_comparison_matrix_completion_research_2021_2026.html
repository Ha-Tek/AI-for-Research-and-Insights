Axiomatic Analysis & Rank Reversal Mitigation

A critical vulnerability in preference modeling is an ordinal violation (rank reversal), where derived weight vectors contradict established transitive orderings implicit in direct comparisons. Research establishes that global consistency index minimizations (EVM + CR, LLSM + GCI) systematically produce rank reversals when n ≥ 7 on Connected Directed Acyclic Graphs (CDAGs).

Diagnostic Takeaway: Global inconsistency metrics average error across the entire matrix, allowing localized path distortions. LOC avoids this entirely by optimizing triad inconsistency lexicographically from worst to best.
Standard Method Failure Mode

Under Eigenvector (CR) or LLSM (GCI) minimization, local preference paths (e.g., A > B > C) are often distorted to reduce total global variance. This creates artificial weight boosts for lower-ranked alternatives once matrix size reaches n ≥ 7.

Axiomatic Superiority of LOC

Lexicographically Optimal Completion (LOC) minimizes localized Koczkodaj triad inconsistencies sequentially. Ágoston & Csató proved that LOC paired with EVM or LLSM yields a 100% guarantee of zero ordinal violations on connected acyclic graphs.

Uniqueness Condition Theorem

The necessary and sufficient condition for the uniqueness of the lexicographically optimal completion is that the undirected graph representing known comparisons is fully connected.

Section 4

Fuzzy Logic & Machine Learning Matrix Completion

Matrix completion methodology expands into subjective group decision-making via Fuzzy DEMATEL Imputation and intersects with Machine Learning Low-Rank Matrix Completion (LRMC) paradigms. Understanding the algebraic distinctions prevents methodological misapplications.

MCDA Matrix Completion

  • Reciprocity Constraint: Strict multiplicative reciprocity a_ji = 1/a_ij.
  • Algebraic Rank: Consistent log-transformed matrices have exact Rank-1 structure.
  • Focus: Small, highly structured decision criteria or alternative preference models.

Fuzzy DEMATEL Imputation

  • Subjective Uncertainty: Evaluates missing values using Triangular Fuzzy Numbers (l, m, u).
  • Causal Mapping: Computes total relation matrix T = N(I-N)⁻¹ to derive direct/indirect influence metrics (D+R, D-R).
  • Focus: Group decision systems with vague or missing expert evaluations.

ML Low-Rank (LRMC)

  • Optimization: Nuclear norm minimization ||X||_* via ADMM solvers.
  • Multi-Label Learning: Converts user interaction matrices into pairwise ranking losses.
  • Distributional LRMC: Extends completion to probability distributions via kernel mean embeddings.
Practical Guidelines for Researchers & Practitioners

System Architecture Best-Practice Roadmap

1 Survey Elicitation Design

Do not ask all n(n-1)/2 questions. Design preference queries using the Union of Two Edge-Disjoint Spanning Trees (2n - 2 edges, average degree ~3). This slashes cognitive fatigue while preserving structural dual-path redundancy.

2 Algorithm Selection

Avoid standard global eigenvalue or GCI minimizations when evaluating ordinal priorities. Always select Lexicographically Optimal Completion (LOC) to ensure 100% protection against rank reversals, especially when matrix size n ≥ 7.

3 Post-Completion Validation

When evaluating post-completion validity under EVM, do not use traditional complete-matrix Random Index baseline tables. Use generalized Random Index (RI) thresholds specifically calibrated via Monte Carlo simulations to your exact missingness ratio.
